# Context

The site is live at `everydeckof.cards` with only a construction notice. The author wants to share it now, and a "why?" page is the natural draw before concrete explorers exist. The motivation, in the author's words:

> Why? It's an interesting technical challenge. Inspired by [Every UUID](https://everyuuid.com/) and [Every Rubik's Cube](https://everycube.alen.is/), the immediate sense of "Wait, how does this work?" gnaws at me. Obviously it's impossible to compute them all ahead of time — but let's explore that for a second. Why is that obvious? Because of just how large a number 52! represents. It's more than the atoms in the Earth. So with that as our basis for a thought experiment — if one shuffled deck were represented by one atom, we'd immediately run out of atoms on Earth to represent them, and now there are no atoms left over to make the Earth, the people, or the computers to store said data. So we'll have to come up with a way to assign a number to a specific permutation. It has to be deterministic and reversible, so that you can share a link to a specific permutation. Rendering creates its own challenge — you can't keep them all in memory, for obvious reasons, so you have to generate permutations on demand, at a very high rate, and render them to the screen as needed — a technique called virtualization. It's also the kind of thing you can show off, or write conference talks about, or just get motivated to actually ship something and see it through. It's a chance to explore a space other than what I do in my day job. It seemed like fun. Also, I have a thing for cards (insert senior picture of me doing a card spring). But mostly just to prove that I could.

The page adapts this text (lightly edited for the page, not rewritten into marketing copy) and pairs it with a scale calculation the domain can actually verify: 52! compared against the estimated number of atoms on Earth (~1.3×10^50, i.e. 52! is roughly 60,000 times larger). Scale claims must expose their assumptions and be reproducible per the architecture documentation rule.

# Decisions Made

- Routing: `@solidjs/router` with a shared `Layout` (masthead/nav/footer) and `<Route>` entries for `/` and `/why`; navigation uses `<A>` so links transition client-side without a page reload. The initial implementation used a `<Switch>` pathname branch with plain anchors, which caused a full document reload on every navigation — immediately jarring in practice. The router dependency (1.0.0) is justified even at two routes, and the plan's original "no new dependencies" instinct was wrong for navigation UX.
- The atoms-on-Earth estimate uses the commonly cited ~1.3×10^50 figure with its source stated inline in the domain module; the comparison is exact bigint arithmetic and the ratio (620,447,501,314,952,912 decks per atom) is pinned by test.
- The card-spring photograph ships as a local `public/card-spring.jpg` asset. Alt text carries the full description ("The author performing a card spring for his senior photos, circa 2009."); the visible caption is just "circa 2009" — the image speaks for itself and the understatement is intentional.

# Deviations

- Initial implementation used `Array#map`, an early-return switch, and `role="img"` on the placeholder; lint (196 rules) correctly rejected all three. Final code uses Solid `<For>`/`<A>` idioms and a plain labelled `figure`.
- The no-dependency routing decision was reversed during review: plain anchors caused full page reloads, so `@solidjs/router` was added and the switch-based `App.tsx` was removed. Recorded above as the durable routing decision.
- The card-spring photo arrived mid-implementation and replaced the planned placeholder, with the author choosing minimal visible caption ("circa 2009") and full context in the alt text.
